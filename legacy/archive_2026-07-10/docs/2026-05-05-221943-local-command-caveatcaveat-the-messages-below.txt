● Answer: how the model "sees" the footprint, and a real bug I just found

  Two paths. The footprint enters the model in two completely separate places:

  1. As a CLIP image embedding (global cross-attention):
  self.fp_encoder(self.fp) produces a 768-d vector that's summed with the
  image-CLIP vector and fed via c_crossattn to every UNet block. This gives the
  model a coarse "what kind of footprint this is" signal but no spatial
  alignment.
  2. As a 3D conditioning volume (spatial concat): _build_fp3d_for lifts the 2D
  footprint into a (B, 1, D, H, W) volume that's concatenated as an extra input
  channel of the UNet (configurable via c_concat, with conditioning_key: hybrid
  in the YAML). This is the path that's supposed to give voxel-level alignment —
   at every latent voxel (z, y, x), the UNet sees fp3d[z, y, x] and learns
  "build solid here / leave empty here."

  Path #2 is where the bug lives.

  Empirical setup I just ran (results above):
  - Loaded COMMERCIALcastle_mesh0365, computed silhouettes by collapsing each
  SDF axis. Confirmed h5['footprint'] == (sdf<=0).any(axis=1) — the stored
  footprint is the top-down view (collapse Y, the up-axis).
  - Encoded the SDF through the VQVAE and decoded back. The reconstructed
  shape's silhouette has IoU 0.83 with the original top-down (collapse Y) and
  only 0.64 with collapse-Z or collapse-X. This proves the VQVAE preserves SDF
  axis ordering: latent (D, H, W) ↔ SDF (z, y, x). The grep over
  vqvae_modules.py confirms this — the only reshape is in attention, and it puts
   axes back at line 174.

  So the latent's axes mean (D=z, H=y, W=x).

  The bug in _build_fp3d_for (models/sdfusion_model_img2shape.py:221):
  fp2d_lat = F.interpolate(fp2d, size=(H, W), mode='nearest')   # resize (z, x) 
  silhouette to (y_lat, x_lat)
  fp3d = fp2d_lat.unsqueeze(2).repeat(1, C, D, 1, 1)            # repeat along 
  axis 2 (latent z)
  This lays the silhouette on axes (3, 4) = (latent_y, latent_x) and replicates
  it along axis 2 = latent_z. So the model is being told: "the footprint
  occupies the y–x plane, identical at every z slice." That's not a floor plan —
   that's a sideways profile, repeated along the building's long axis.
  Effectively a 90° rotation around X.

  The cubic latent (16×16×16) hides the bug numerically — no shape mismatches,
  training loss decreases just fine — but the ground-plan ↔ shape coupling is
  destroyed. The model is learning some consistent mapping (which is why loss
  falls), but the spatial guidance you intended ("build at these (z,x)
  positions") isn't what the model is actually receiving.

  The fix (one function, two-line change):
  fp2d_lat = F.interpolate(fp2d, size=(D, W), mode='nearest')   # 
  (B,1,z_lat,x_lat)
  fp3d = fp2d_lat.unsqueeze(3).repeat(1, C, 1, H, 1)            # (B,C,D=z, H=y,
   W=x)
  Now fp3d[b, c, z, y, x] == silhouette[z, x] for all y, which is what
  "footprint" means.

  What this changes for you:
  - Old runs (the 100K-step one and this smoke run) trained against a rotated
  footprint. Their geometry-vs-footprint coupling is wrong even where loss looks
   healthy.
  - After the fix, the F2/F3 fixes you already have plus correct axis alignment
  should give the model the actual signal you wanted.
  - inference() calls the same helper — same fix applies.

  The smoke test currently in flight is on the broken axis. Two options:

  1. Apply F8 now, kill the current smoke, restart a fresh smoke. Best cycle
  time, and it confirms the fix works.
  2. Let the current smoke finish (~iter 1750, hit milestone iter 500 with loss

  I lean (1) — the current smoke has already shown loss is logging cleanly and

  - c_img = CLIPImageEncoder(footprint_png) → cross-attention
  - c_fp = CLIPImageEncoder(footprint_png) → cross-attention (added to c_img)

  What GeoTexBuild does that solves our problem

  Their key technical move is the missing piece for our project: a height map as
   the second conditioning signal. It's a single-channel grayscale image, same
  (z, x) projection as the footprint, where each pixel encodes the maximum
  building height at that ground location.

  This is exactly the right disambiguation for the one-to-many "footprint → 3D"
  problem I described: a footprint says where, a height map says how tall 
  everywhere. Together they are much closer to determining a building. And
  critically, both are derivable from your existing SDF data — no rendering
  pipeline needed.

  Their pipeline:

  1. Stage 1: footprint + grid-of-height-hints → ControlNet-finetuned-from-SD1.5
   → height map (trained on Building3D, 32k buildings, 260k aug pairs, 62h on
  a 3090)
  2. Stage 2: footprint + height map → mesh via classical ops (bilateral filter,
   polygon-fit, Poisson, booleans). Non-neural.
  3. Stage 3: Text2Mesh for color + displacement.

  Their own paper admits the limitation:

  ▎ "End-to-end direct 3D generative models trained on comprehensive 3D datasets
  ▎  with extensive GPU resources may yield superior fine geometry and texture 
  ▎ resolution. However, constraints related to data formats and computational 
  ▎ capacity have precluded effective geometric control in feedforward models."

  That gap — "we'd love to use a real 3D generator but couldn't" — is exactly 
  where our SDFusion sits. So the cleanest fit is: steal their idea, not their 
  architecture.

  Concrete proposal — F9: height-map conditioning for SDFusion

  Two pieces, one is novel relative to GeoTexBuild:

  Piece 1 — augment SDFusion with a GT height-map channel (trivial, big payoff)

  - For each <id>/ori_sample_grid.h5, compute height_map[z, x] = max_y(sdf<=0)
  (normalized to [0,1]). Single-channel, 1×64×64. Reuses the same axis fix as
  F8.
  - Write a scripts/recompute_heightmaps_from_sdf.py that adds height to each h5
   (mirrors what we did for footprints).

  → c_concat becomes 2-channel.
  - Bump UNet in_channels from 4 to 5 in configs/sdfusion-img2shape.yaml.
  - Drop the redundant img_encoder (which was reading the same footprint PNG
  anyway). Cleaner model.

  shaped much closer to actual buildings instead of category-mean blobs.

  - a house (low, pitched roof, residential)
  - a church (cruciform, tall central nave, sometimes a steeple)
  - a factory (flat, large roof, no setbacks)
  - etc.

  Footprint + height map alone narrows it to "rectangular and ~3 stories tall,"
  but doesn't tell the model whether it's an office or a temple. That's exactly
  why even with F9 the model would still average over building kinds whose
  tall-rectangle silhouettes overlap.

  (b) The label is already in the data. BuildingNet model IDs encode both
  top-level category and subtype — for free:

  - 5 top-level: RESIDENTIAL (986), RELIGIOUS (368), COMMERCIAL (99), MILITARY

  - ~25+ subtypes worth using: house, villa, church, mosque, office_building,

  school_building, …

  cover ~95% of the dataset.

  Proposal: F9.5 — class conditioning alongside the height map

  Add a categorical signal to the cross-attention stream so the model knows what
   kind of building to produce. Implementation:

  1. Dataset loader: regex-parse the model_id at load time → (category, subtype)
   ids. ~10 lines.
  2. Class embedding: small nn.Embedding(num_classes, 768) so it shares
  dimensionality with the existing CLIP cross-attention. Add it to the
  cross-attention input alongside (or replacing) the image encoder.
  3. Classifier-free guidance: at training, drop the class label with
  probability ~0.1 (sub it with a null embedding). At inference, set CFG scale
  to amplify the class signal. Standard recipe; we already have uc_scale
  plumbing.
  4. Granularity choice: start with the 5 top-level classes (clean, balanced
  enough). Expose subtypes as an option if you want; can also condition on
  (top_level, subtype) jointly.

  Combined with F9 (height map), each training tuple now becomes:

  (footprint, height_map, class_label) → SDF

  Three signals encoding where, how tall, what kind. That's a fully-determined
  input the model can actually learn from. The blob problem you saw isn't just a
   long-training-time issue — it's that we were giving the model an
  underdetermined input. Each missing axis (alignment, height, class) was a
  degree of freedom the model had to average over.

  How this plays with your end-goal pipeline

  For the multi-building map use case:

  map → footprints → for each footprint:
                       class label  ← user-supplied OR small classifier (F9.5b)
                       height map   ← predicted by tiny U-Net (F9 piece 2)
                       SDF          ← SDFusion(footprint, height, class)
                     placed mesh

  Two ways the user provides class at inference:

  - (i) Manual labels in the map (e.g., GeoJSON {"type": "church"} per polygon).
   Easiest. For a poster figure you'd want this anyway.
  - (ii) Tiny classifier: footprint + (optional context: neighborhood density,
  polygon area) → class probabilities. Trivial to add — another small "novel
  thing we train" if you want one. ~2M params, trains in minutes.

  Three paths to consider, in increasing ambition

  Path R — Retrieval baseline (cheap, robust, good for your map use case)

  Given a footprint:
  1. Compute footprint shape descriptor (Hu moments, Fourier descriptors, or a
  CLIP embedding of the silhouette).
  2. Nearest-neighbor lookup against the 1470 BuildingNet training silhouettes.
  3. Take the matching mesh, deform its footprint to match the input via
  cage-based deformation (Laplacian or biharmonic) — single closed-form solve,
  no learning.

  Pros: always produces a clean mesh (it is a real mesh, deformed). Zero-blob
  guarantee. Trivial to wire into the multi-building map pipeline.
  Cons: limited to shapes already in the dataset. No novelty for a paper.
  Novel small thing: none worth writing about.
  Effort: half a day total.

  Path Q — Frozen pretrained 3D generator + small trainable front-end (highest 
  quality, recommended)

  Drop the SDFusion training entirely. Replace with:

  1. Tiny trainable model: footprint (+ class label + maybe height map) → an
  orthographic side-view image of a building consistent with that footprint.
  ControlNet-from-SD1.5 finetuned on BuildingNet renders. ~30k steps, half a day
   on Gilbreth.
  2. Frozen Hunyuan3D-2 (or Trellis): that image → high-quality mesh.
  3. Per-polygon, normalize to map coords, place.

  This is essentially GeoTexBuild's stage 1, but routed into a real 2024-era 3D 
  generator instead of their non-neural mesh fitting. The novelty:
  domain-specific footprint→reference-view ControlNet for buildings, paired with
   a state-of-the-art geometry model — they don't combine those.

  Pros: end-to-end mesh quality jumps drastically. We keep training only the
  front-end (where BuildingNet is plenty of data). Inference is seconds per
  building.
  Cons: geometry is constrained by what the pretrained model "knows" about
  buildings (Hunyuan3D-2's training data is generic, includes some buildings).
  Output mesh isn't perfectly footprint-locked unless we add a refinement step.
  Novel small thing: the footprint(+class)→building-view ControlNet, trained on
  BuildingNet renders. Real research contribution.
  Effort: ~1–1.5 days of setup + train + integrate. ~$0 in compute (frozen
  models on local GPU).

  Path S — Stay on SDFusion, do F9 + F9.5

  Everything we already discussed. Lower upper-bound on quality (small dataset,
  older arch), but the work is partly done and the contribution is "end-to-end
  footprint+height+class-conditioned 3D diffusion."

  What I think the right call is

  Path Q is the highest-quality answer and also the one that most cleanly
  produces the small novel trainable component you want. We're trying to compete
   with models that have 100× the training data — by using their geometry

  Three places SDFs naturally live in this pipeline

  [footprint + class]        ← user-supplied per polygon on the map

  BuildingNet renders

          ↓

          ↓
      marching cubes → mesh per polygon
          ↓
      SDF-union scene composition  (free)
          ↓
  [single clean mesh of the whole map]

  Three SDF placements:

  1. Hunyuan3D-2's mesh output → SDF (voxelize). Mesh composition across many
  buildings is messy (t-junctions, non-manifolds); SDF composition is min(SDF_i)
   over all buildings in world coords, then marching cubes once. This alone is a
   justifiable novelty for the multi-building map use case.
  2. SDF-residual refinement model (the headline novel piece). A small 3D
  diffusion model — exactly the SDFusion architecture we already have — trained
  NOT to generate from scratch but to learn the residual that turns
  Hunyuan3D-2's prediction into a footprint-faithful, class-matching SDF.
  3. Footprint-as-SDF hard constraint (free): compute analytic SDF of the
  footprint polygon, extrude to height map, and clamp the refined SDF to never
  extend beyond it. Hard guarantee that no generated geometry pokes outside the
  polygon — important for the multi-building map use case so adjacent buildings
  don't overlap.

  Why the SDF-residual is the right place to be novel

  GeoTexBuild does footprint→height→classical-mesh; their output is locked into
  what bilateral-filter + Poisson can produce. Hunyuan3D-2 alone does
  image→mesh; its output knows nothing about your footprint or building class.
  Neither uses SDF at the refinement step.

  Our novelty: "large pretrained 3D generators are strong on visual style but 
  weak on domain-specific structural constraints; we close that gap with a small
   SDF-residual diffusion model conditioned on footprint, height, and class."
  That's a real, publishable contribution and it reuses the VQVAE+SDFusion code
  we've already debugged.

  The training is also much easier than from-scratch SDFusion:
  - Target: GT_SDF − Hunyuan3D-2_SDF (the residual)
  - Distribution of residuals is much narrower than distribution of full SDFs
  - 1470 training pairs is enough because we're learning a correction, not the
  whole shape distribution
  - Probably 10K-20K steps to converge, vs the 100K+ needed for from-scratch
